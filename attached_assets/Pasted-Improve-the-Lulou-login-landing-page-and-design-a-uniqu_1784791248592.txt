PART 3 — CREATE THE LULOU CONNECTION DNA SYSTEM

Build a unique matching system called:

Lulou Connection DNA

The goal is to match users based on how they are likely to connect in real life, not just on who they visually like.

The algorithm must combine:

- Onboarding compatibility
- User preferences
- Behavioural learning
- Conversation quality signals
- Call outcomes
- Continued mutual interest
- Intentional variety

Do not create a simple personality-label system.

Do not assign users simplistic labels such as “introvert”, “romantic” or “type A”.

Instead, create a multidimensional compatibility profile.

PART 4 — CONNECTION DNA QUIZ

After account creation and the required onboarding steps, every user should complete a short compatibility experience.

The quiz should feel engaging and personal, not clinical or exhausting.

Requirements:

- Approximately 15 to 20 questions
- Mobile-friendly
- One question per screen
- Progress indicator
- Save progress between screens
- Allow users to continue if they close the app
- Require completion before full Discover access
- Explain why the questions are being asked
- Allow answers to be updated later in Settings

Measure dimensions including:

- Relationship intention
- Communication style
- Emotional openness
- Affection style
- Social energy
- Lifestyle rhythm
- Ambition and priorities
- Conflict style
- Dating pace
- Independence versus togetherness
- Future goals
- Spontaneity versus structure
- Depth versus playfulness
- Ideal frequency of communication
- Availability for dating

Use realistic scenario-based questions.

Avoid obvious or easily gamed questions.

Bad example:
“Are you a good communicator?”

Better example:
“Someone you are dating becomes quieter than usual. What would you naturally do?”

Possible answers:
- Ask directly if something is wrong
- Give them space and check in later
- Try to lighten the mood
- Wait for them to bring it up
- Feel uncertain and become quieter too

Another example:

“You have one completely free Saturday. Which sounds most appealing?”

Possible answers:
- A spontaneous day out
- A planned activity and dinner
- A quiet day together at home
- Time apart followed by an evening together
- Seeing friends as a couple

Create a weighted response model that converts answers into compatibility dimensions.

PART 5 — MATCHING DIMENSIONS

Create internal numerical scores for each user across dimensions such as:

- Relationship seriousness
- Communication directness
- Emotional depth
- Affection expression
- Social energy
- Lifestyle pace
- Ambition
- Independence
- Conflict repair
- Planning style
- Spontaneity
- Dating pace
- Future alignment
- Playfulness
- Availability

Store these securely and do not expose raw internal scores publicly.

Only show users tasteful summaries and match explanations.

PART 6 — INITIAL MATCHING FORMULA

Use the following starting balance:

- 30% relationship values and intentions
- 20% communication and emotional compatibility
- 15% lifestyle compatibility
- 10% dating pace and availability
- 10% mutual preferences
- 10% learned behaviour and interaction outcomes
- 5% intentional variety and serendipity

The exact implementation may use normalized component scores.

Do not rely on one total score alone.

Store component-level compatibility so the product can explain why two people were introduced.

PART 7 — BEHAVIOURAL LEARNING

Lulou should learn from user behaviour, but must not become another appearance-only swipe algorithm.

Use signals including:

Lower-value signals:
- Profile viewed
- Time spent reading prompts
- Full profile opened
- Like
- Pass

Medium-value signals:
- Mutual match
- Conversation started
- Replies continued
- Both users reached 10 messages
- Voice notes unlocked

High-value signals:
- Both users reached the first call stage
- Call completed
- Both users chose to continue
- Conversation progressed to later stages
- Both users selected Plan date
- Date was confirmed, where that feature exists

The strongest algorithm signals must come from mutual progression and real interaction outcomes, not simple likes.

A like alone must have limited influence.

A pass should not permanently eliminate an entire personality type from future recommendations.

PART 8 — POST-INTERACTION FEEDBACK

After a call, ended conversation or final decision, ask one short private feedback question.

Example:

What felt strongest about this connection?

Options:
- Conversation flowed naturally
- We shared similar values
- I felt attracted to them
- We wanted different things
- Communication felt difficult
- Our lifestyles did not align
- I would like more introductions like this
- I am not sure yet

This feedback must:

- Remain private
- Never be shown to the other user
- Improve future recommendations
- Not unfairly punish the other user
- Be used as a soft learning signal rather than an absolute label
- Include abuse safeguards and rate limits

Do not expose private negative feedback to users.

PART 9 — EXPLAINABLE MATCHING

Every recommended profile should include a section called:

Why Lulou introduced you

Examples:

“You both value direct communication, prefer committed relationships and enjoy a similar social pace.”

“A different kind of match: your personalities differ, but your relationship values and future priorities strongly align.”

Show two or three meaningful reasons.

Do not show percentages that imply scientific certainty.

Do not show sensitive inferred traits.

Do not reveal internal raw scores.

The explanation should be:
- Positive
- Specific
- Easy to understand
- Grounded in answers and behaviour
- Never manipulative

PART 10 — PREVENT AN ALGORITHM BUBBLE

The discovery feed must not narrow users into one repetitive visual or personality type.

Use a recommendation mix approximately like:

- 70% strong compatibility recommendations
- 20% promising people slightly outside the user’s usual behaviour pattern
- 10% carefully selected wildcard introductions

A wildcard must not be random.

It should still satisfy:
- Hard preference requirements
- Age and distance settings
- Gender and orientation settings
- Safety rules
- Relationship intention compatibility
- Block and exclusion rules

Wildcard profiles may differ from the user’s usual interaction pattern while still showing strong underlying compatibility.

PART 11 — CONNECTION COMPASS

Create a user-facing feature called:

Connection Compass

This should gradually show users what appears to support their strongest connections.

Possible dimensions:

- Communication
- Emotional depth
- Lifestyle
- Playfulness
- Ambition
- Relationship pace

Do not show this immediately.

Require enough interaction data before displaying insights.

Do not pretend to know anything when there is insufficient data.

Examples of safe insights:

“You often like highly social profiles, but your strongest conversations have happened with people who prefer quieter, more intentional dates.”

“You tend to progress further with people who communicate directly and want a similar relationship pace.”

“This insight is still developing as Lulou learns from more of your genuine interactions.”

The feature must not:
- Diagnose users
- Insult users
- Rank their attractiveness
- Reveal another user’s private feedback
- Make overly confident claims from limited data

PART 12 — COLD START LOGIC

For new users with no behavioural history:

- Use Connection DNA quiz results
- Use stated preferences
- Use relationship intention
- Use location and availability
- Use a controlled variety mix
- Avoid over-personalising too early

As interaction data grows, gradually increase the behavioural-learning component.

Do not allow early likes and passes to overpower the original compatibility profile.

PART 13 — FAIRNESS AND SAFETY

The algorithm must never use or infer protected or highly sensitive personal traits for ranking unless required by an explicit user-controlled dating preference and legally permitted.

Do not rank users based on:
- Race
- Religion
- Disability
- Income assumptions
- Health conditions
- Political beliefs
- Device type
- Subscription status
- Whether they paid for boosts

Paid features may increase visibility only where clearly disclosed.

They must not falsely change compatibility scores.

Include:
- Blocked user exclusion
- Reported user safety controls
- Suspended and paused account exclusion
- Under-18 exclusion
- Geographic and legal restrictions
- Spam and bot detection
- Repeated negative-behaviour safeguards

PART 14 — USER CONTROL

Allow users to:

- Retake or edit Connection DNA answers
- Adjust hard preferences
- See why a profile was recommended
- Reduce or reset behavioural personalisation
- Request more variety
- Hide Connection Compass insights
- Disable optional behavioural personalisation where required
- Delete their algorithm-related data through account deletion and data export flows

Clearly distinguish:

Hard filters:
- Gender preference
- Age range
- Distance
- Relationship intention where required
- Blocking and safety rules

Soft compatibility preferences:
- Communication
- Lifestyle
- Social energy
- Ambition
- Affection
- Dating pace

PART 16 — ALGORITHM VERSIONING

Add algorithm versioning so future changes do not silently corrupt old scores.

Requirements:

- Save the algorithm version with calculated profiles and recommendations
- Recalculate safely when the model changes
- Log component scores internally for debugging
- Do not log sensitive raw answers in production logs
- Add feature flags so the new algorithm can be tested gradually
- Support A/B testing without changing hard safety rules

PART 17 — ANALYTICS

Measure whether the system creates better real connections.

Track privacy-safe aggregate metrics such as:

- Profile view to like rate
- Match to conversation-start rate
- Conversation to 10-each rate
- Voice-note unlock rate
- First-call unlock rate
- Call completion rate
- Continue-after-call rate
- Plan-date rate
- Unmatch or report rate
- Time to meaningful progression
- Recommendation diversity

Do not optimise solely for time spent in the app.

The primary success metric should be meaningful mutual progression toward real dates.

PART 18 — PERFORMANCE

Do not calculate expensive compatibility scores on every profile render.

Use:
- Precomputed or cached compatibility
- Incremental updates
- Background recalculation where appropriate
- Indexed database fields
- Pagination
- Batching
- Realtime updates only when needed

Ensure Discover remains fast on iPhone Safari and lower-powered mobile devices.
